01 — About

Three teams, one standard.

Team N!TRO represents The British School, New Delhi in STEM Racing — the competition most people still know as F1 in Schools. Each season starts with a block of polyurethane foam and a CO₂ cartridge, and ends with the fastest car we can defend in front of judges.

The work runs wider than the car: research, CAD, CFD, manufacture, testing, branding, enterprise and a presentation that has to hold up to questioning. In August 2026, N!TRO, Energise and Radeon merged — three rosters reduced to five specialists, under one rule: every detail must have a reason.

BOCA hybrid bearings

The race is won
in the bearings.

Ceramic balls running in steel races: harder, rounder and lighter than steel on steel, with less friction to lose as heat. The gain on any single run is small. Across a full round of heats, it is the difference between a fast car and a consistent one.

03 — Process

From question to car.

Four stages, run in order, every season. Nothing moves forward until the stage before it survives being questioned.

01

Research

The rulebook sets the boundaries. Last season's race data sets the targets.

02

Design

Parametric CAD, so nothing is drawn twice. Change one constraint, the whole body follows.

03

Test

CFD, smoke testing, then physical trials. The model stays a hypothesis until the track agrees.

04

Manufacture

CNC-machined foam and MJF-printed PA12 — where tolerances stop being theoretical.
